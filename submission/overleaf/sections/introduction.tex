\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Mean reversion is a central organizing principle in continuous-time finance.
The Gaussian specification of \citet{Vasicek1977} and the square-root
diffusion of \citet{CoxIngersollRoss1985} remain canonical because they
combine an interpretable restoring force with tractable transition laws.
Empirical financial dynamics, however, are often nonlinear and state
dependent.  \citet{ChanEtAl1992} compare alternative short-rate diffusions,
\citet{AitSahalia1996} documents nonlinear drift and volatility, and
\citet{BibbySorensen1995}, \citet{Kessler1997}, and
\citet{AitSahalia2002} develop inference for discretely observed diffusions.
These findings motivate models that retain a precise restoring mechanism
without forcing the marginal distribution and temporal dependence into one
Gaussian specification.

Copulas provide that separation.  \citet{DarsowNguyenOlsen1992} connect
Copulas and Markov transition operators through the star product, while
\citet{ChenFan2006} and \citet{ChenWuYi2009} develop semiparametric
estimation for stationary Copula-based Markov models.  Monotone
space--time transformations provide a complementary construction
\citep{BibbonaSacerdoteTorre2016}, and \citet{FangJiangLiuYang2020} study
Copula-based Markov processes directly.  This literature establishes how to
separate margins from serial dependence.  It does not make stationarity, a
declining dependence coefficient, and an economic restoring force
interchangeable.  We therefore formulate mean reversion through conditional
drift and conditional-mean restrictions and state separately what is needed
for convergence under a chosen initial law.

Stablecoins provide a sharp application because the state of interest is the
deviation from a peg.  The survey of \citet{AnteEtAl2023} documents a broad
and rapidly growing empirical literature.  \citet{LyonsViswanathNatraj2023}
connect Tether's peg stability to the design and accessibility of arbitrage,
while \citet{DuanUrquhart2023} find that correction speeds vary across
stablecoins and over time.  Nonlinear or time-varying stabilization also
appears in \citet{DjogbenouInanJasiak2023} and
\citet{HuiWongLo2025}; \citet{BergaultEtAl2024} model pegged assets with
nested OU factors.  Run incentives and tail events add another dimension
\citep{MaZengZhang2023,LiuMakarovSchoar2023,AhmedAldasoroDuley2025,
EichengreenNguyenViswanathNatraj2025}.  A useful model must therefore
represent ordinary correction and exceptional transitions without confusing
a better short-horizon density fit with a credible long-run distribution.

The paper develops three process families.  Section~2 gives an absolutely
continuous Markov--Copula foundation and exact conditional-mean and generator
criteria.  Section~3 constructs observable processes through strictly
monotone transformations of OU and CIR diffusions.  The inverse map and
Jacobian make explicit when a formal PDE solution does not define an
estimable model because monotonicity, support, or invertibility fails.
Section~4 introduces the central stationary-reset construction.  A
multiplicative mixture of a stationary transition Copula and independence is
a genuine Markov evolution, preserves its invariant margin, and multiplies
conditional-mean persistence by a survival probability.  Its nonhomogeneous
Poisson representation places the result within the stochastic-resetting
literature \citep{EvansMajumdar2011,EvansMajumdarSchehr2020}; the contribution
here is not the reset mechanism itself, but its Markov--Copula formulation,
margin preservation, observable density, and exact reversion identities.

Section~5 uses a common-exchange, common-calendar design for Kraken USDT/USD,
USDC/USD, and DAI/USD from 2021 through 2025.  All assets are processed with
the same gap rule, price-quantization model, likelihood, training window, and
scoring protocol.  This is a mechanism-aligned heterogeneity exercise rather
than a causal design: a common venue and synchronized calendar reduce, but do
not remove, confounding from liquidity and asset-specific trading intensity.
One-minute data are used to test microstructure sensitivity and continuous-time
semigroup consistency rather than simply to enlarge the sample.

The observation model changes the empirical problem.  Kraken transaction
prices lie on tick grids and a large fraction of adjacent closes are exactly
equal.  With even one tie, the continuous mixed-OU point-density likelihood is
unbounded as the base innovation variance collapses, while the reset component
continues to assign positive mass to non-ties.  We replace that criterion by
the probability of the observed price interval.  We then separate full-sample
descriptive estimates from frozen training estimates and attach day-clustered
uncertainty to out-of-sample score differences.  The mixed representation
improves five-minute prediction for the two most liquid assets, but its
estimated rates change materially between one- and five-minute data.  The
paper therefore treats dependence refresh as an empirically useful
representation while withholding a structural interpretation of
$\kappa$ and $\lambda$ until full filtering and cross-frequency restrictions
are passed.
