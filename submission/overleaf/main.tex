\title{Copula-Based Mean-Reverting Processes:\\
Theory, Observation Discreteness, and Stablecoin Evidence}
\author{Qinhan Duan \and Qian Gao \and Jingping Yang \and Jiaqi Zhu}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We develop a copula-based framework for mean-reverting Markov processes and
evaluate its empirical content using one- and five-minute Kraken prices for
USDT/USD, USDC/USD, and DAI/USD.
The theory distinguishes local drift reversion, finite-horizon conditional
mean reversion, and convergence of moments under an initial law.  For
absolutely continuous Markov copulas, we derive conditional-mean and generator
criteria, establish observable densities for monotone transforms of OU and CIR
diffusions, and characterize a stationary-reset family that preserves its
invariant marginal while multiplying centered semigroup decay by a survival
probability.  The empirical analysis first identifies a decisive observation
problem: 17--54 percent of aligned five-minute transitions are exact price
ties, making the usual continuous mixed-OU point-density likelihood unbounded
as the base reversion rate approaches zero.  We therefore introduce a bounded
nearest-tick interval likelihood and separate 2021--2025 descriptive estimates
from parameters frozen on 2021--2023.  The three assets share 229,484 exact,
non-imputed five-minute transition times.  Under the interval approximation,
the mixed model improves 2024 and post-selection 2025 scores relative to OU
for USDT and USDC; DAI evidence is less stable.  One-to-five-minute parameter
transfer fails for all three assets, however, so the continuous-time semigroup
interpretation and cross-asset parameter ordering do not pass the present
structural gate.  The evidence supports a useful dependence-refresh
representation, but full filtering, synchronous multi-asset inference, and
frequency stability are required before its rates can be given a structural
economic interpretation.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} mean reversion; Markov copula; stablecoin; transition density; stationary reset; invariant margin
